# Results: Context, the play-outcome forecaster (`context-live`)

**Date:** 2026-11-17 · **Brief:** [BRIEF.md](BRIEF.md) · **Exploration:** [EXPLORATION.md](EXPLORATION.md) · **Decision:** [0036](../../docs/decisions/0036-context-live.md) · **MLflow experiment:** `context-live` · **Champion:** `pcf.context-live` version 1 (run `72a7e2e3a3c44faead7fc41cd6dc8032`: each outcome's shipped rung refit on 2022-2025, trained through 2026-02-22; its 2026 forecasts, weeks 1-8 so far, are `outputs/context-live/72a7e2e3.../live.parquet`, remade each week with `run live --version 1`)

## In short
For every scrimmage play from 2023, the expert gives 17 expected outcomes from the situation and both sides' calls, with nothing about who played. The calls beat the situation-only model, by more than the fitting noise, for 12 of the 17. They matter most for how a pass is thrown: time to throw (1.3% to 17.4% of squared error removed), depth of target (4.3% to 18.2%), pressure (1.0% to 4.2%) and completion (1.6% to 3.9%). For a run's yards and for how a play ends they add a few tenths of a point. Five outcomes ship the situation-only model: a stuffed run, a run's first down and touchdown, a dropback's touchdown, and penalties. Every shipped outcome beats all 20 of both nulls and is calibrated (after recalibrating two). Its ranges hold 79% to 82%. On the vault (2025) every shipped outcome keeps its signal and about its size. The one miss is pressure's calibration slope (0.89 against a bar of 0.9) as 2025's pressure rate fell.

Then the vault run, and the champion (a first champion run failed to save its model and registered nothing: decision 0036, call 19). ## The CEO's spot-check: why the on-field map can't be a call field (2026-11-19)
The CEO, word for word: *"For the context expert, I think you can spot check A, why the who was on the field map can't be an input. I think I'm a little confused about what you're talking about. If you give me a refresher, then I can be more specific about what to spot check. But I think if you just spot check that in general and give me a report back on what you find and the constraints, limitations, advantages, and the outstanding work, then that's good."*

Script: [`explore/spotcheck_map_leak.py`](explore/spotcheck_map_leak.py); output: `explore/spotcheck_map_leak.txt`.

**Refresher: what the map is.** The free play-by-play doesn't say who was on the field. It names only who touched the ball or made the play: the passer, the target or ball carrier, the tacklers, the sacker, a pass defender. The full list of all 22 players arrives months later, after the season (the participation data). `participation-late` fills that gap: for every play it gives each player a chance he was on the field, from his snap counts, his position's usual rotation, and the names the play-by-play gives. Adding a play's chances by position gives its expected personnel, for example 2.8 wide receivers, 1.3 tight ends and 1.0 back. That was the brief's step 5, the CEO's "3 wide receivers", known two weeks after the game.

**What "leak" means here.** This expert's inputs may say only how the play was set up, never how it ended: it is the "expected" that a player's actual result is measured against. But the play-by-play's names are part of how the play ended. The map puts every named player on the field at a chance of exactly 1, and because a play has 11 players a side, someone else's chance goes down. So a play where a linebacker made the sack has a touch more linebackers in the map's count than one where nobody was sacked. A model that reads the map's personnel learns that pattern, and its "forecast" of the sack is partly read off the sack.

**What the check found** (70,376 runs and dropbacks in 2023-2024 that have both the map and the true list):
1. **The map is certain about every named player and only fairly sure about the rest.** Named players: chance 1.000, all 144,666 of them. Players who were on the field but weren't named: 0.79 on average, and 39% at 1.
2. **Its counts lean toward the players who made the play.** Where a defensive lineman was named, the map counts 0.085 linemen too many; where none was, 0.03 too few. Linebackers +0.06 / −0.03, defensive backs +0.05 / −0.04, backs +0.04 / −0.04; receivers and tight ends hardly (+0.01, +0.02). On sacks the map adds linemen (+0.07) and drops defensive backs (−0.09) against the truth; on other dropbacks it is off by 0.01.
3. **The "gain" from the map's personnel is all leak, and the honest gain is nothing.** Added to the live model (2024, fit on 2023), the true personnel changes the loss by −0.18% to +0.14% across seven outcomes: noise. The map's personnel "improves" it by +0.27% (run success) to +1.71% (depth of target). If personnel itself mattered, the truth would help at least as much as the map's guess of it.

| Outcome | With the true personnel | With the map's personnel |
|---|---|---|
| depth of target | −0.01% of the loss | +1.71% |
| completion | +0.14% | +0.85% |
| sack | −0.09% | +1.03% |
| pressure | +0.10% | +0.48% |
| rushing yards | +0.11% | +0.67% |
| run success | −0.18% | +0.27% |
| dropback success | +0.04% | +0.56% |

**Why the truth adds nothing:** FTN's call fields already carry the part of personnel that matters for these outcomes: the backs in the backfield, the box count, the pass rushers, shotgun, the quarterback's spot. "Eleven personnel" adds nothing a model can use beyond them.

- **Constraints.** The true list is after-the-season data, so it can be a check here but never an in-season input. Both are needed to compare, so the check covers 2023-2024, with one test season.
- **Limitations.** Seven outcomes were checked, not all seventeen (the others are the same populations' first downs, touchdowns, explosives and penalties). The answer is for this expert's job, describing a play after the game. Personnel could still matter for another job: forecasting what a team will call before the snap, or a player's role and snap share (participation's own job).
- **Advantages of leaving it out.** No leak. The expert stays "the setup, not the players", which is what makes it fair to measure players against. It doesn't depend on participation-late's model or its version.
- **What it means for the player context (R9).** Each player's context weights plays by the map's chances, so a player is placed on his own named plays with certainty, and on the rest at 0.79 or so. Over 200+ plays a season that makes a busy receiver's or tackler's context sharper, not better or worse. It isn't measured; the after-the-season edition (`context-retro`) can use the true list instead.
- **Outstanding work.** None for this expert. Idea I63 (a map that doesn't pin the play's own names, then personnel as a call field) is answered without building it: even perfect personnel adds nothing, so it is killed. For `context-retro`, use the true list, not the map. FTN's "0 pass rushers" (R17, idea I72) is the one input fault left, for the next edition.

## The case against
- **Every gain is the calls, and the calls are charted after the play.** A screen, play action and a trick play are partly what happened, and the box count and blitzers are what FTN saw. The biggest gains (time to throw, depth of target) are where the offense's calls describe the throw itself: a screen is a short, quick throw by definition. So "the context explains 18% of a target's depth" is partly "a screen is short". The brief accepts this (the CEO's call 3), but a later expert crediting a quarterback for depth over expected should know that the expected part already holds his screen calls.
- **Two test seasons, one for pressure and time to throw.** 44 weeks of blocks for most outcomes and 22 for those two. The no-noise rule asks for an interval clear of zero and a gain larger than the fitting noise; with one season, pressure's 2.8% gain rests on 2024 alone, trained on 2023 alone. The vault adds one more season.
- **The choice of what ships, and of which chances to recalibrate, was made on the same two seasons it is reported on.** The calibration fix (sack and explosive pass) was picked by their 2023-2024 slopes. The vault is the clean check (it passed for sack and explosive pass, slopes 0.91 and 1.08; pressure, not recalibrated, slipped to 0.89).
- **The fitting noise is small because the rows are many, not because the gain is large.** Run success ships the trees with a gain of 0.22% of the loss against a noise of 0.12. For a downstream user that is about 0.3 points of success rate on a play.
- **The calls carry talent in part** (the brief's flagged assumption). A light box is earned by the offense's receivers; a blitz is chosen against a weak line. Nothing measures how much here; the exploration found no team identity left at the play level, which bounds it but doesn't rule it out.
- **The vault would have chosen differently for some outcomes.** On 2025 alone the calls beat the situation for three outcomes that ship the situation (a stuffed run, a run's first down, a dropback's touchdown), and don't clear zero for run success, which ships the calls. Each of these gains is 0.1% to 0.7% of the loss: the line between "the calls help" and "they don't" for those outcomes is thinner than one season can settle.
- **The player context uses the on-field map that leaks on single plays** (R11, R18). Averaged over a player's 200+ plays the pins are a small part, but a player who is named often (a busy receiver, a tackler) is placed on his own plays at a chance of 1 and on the rest at about 0.79: his context is measured more sharply, not more favourably. Not measured; `context-retro` can use the true list.

## Pitfalls
- **Pressure and time to throw changed with the data provider in 2023** (decision 0036, call 5). `was_pressure` is charted on 29% of 2022's dropbacks and 15% after; time to throw averages 2.79 s then 2.65 s. The exploration trained 2023's pressure on 2022, which teaches the model the wrong base rate. Here they start in 2023: one season trains 2024, two train the vault.
- **participation-late's on-field chances carry the play's result** (call 14). The map pins every player the play-by-play names (the target, the tackler, the sacker, a pass defender) and balances each play to 11, so a play's expected position counts know who made the play. Added as call fields, they looked like 0.3% to 1.7% of the loss; undoing the pins made the "gain" larger (sack 1.1% to 15.3%), which is the signature of a leak. Any model that uses the map's chances on the same play as its outcome has this problem; a player's average over many plays (this project's player context) is much less exposed, since each play's pins are a small part of it, but not free of it.
- **FTN writes "0" for an uncharted QB location or hash.** Read as a value, it is a category of its own that marks plays FTN didn't fully chart. It is turned into missing here.
- **FTN's "0 pass rushers" is also an uncharted play, and it is still read as a value here** (R17). With a box of about 2, those dropbacks are sacked at the usual rate, which a real 0-man rush can't be: 0.3% to 1.2% of dropbacks a season. Version 1 reads the 0 as a count; the next edition turns it into missing (call 21, idea I72).
- **A few trick plays early in a 3rd quarter make the trees' highest expected rushing yards** (R16). The quarter field isn't the cause (it moves nothing); the trick-play flag and the game clock together are. Read the top 1% of expected rushing yards with care.
- **FTN's own known-at isn't the play-by-play's.** The gateway names it `ftn_known_at`; a play's row is known at the later of the two. The first point-in-time check used the combined known-at and counted 859 "early" FTN rows that weren't; it now compares FTN's rows against the play-by-play spine's known-at (the morning after) and finds 0.
- **The calendar for `as_of` must cover every season read.** A calendar limited to the scored seasons left 2022's playoff games without an `as_of`; the runner now takes the full calendar and refuses a play without one.
- **The trees' mean for an amount over-shoots its top tenth** (rushing yards 6.1 forecast against 5.2 actual). The range score, which is what's scored, doesn't see it; a squared-error check by tenth does (report `mean by tenth`). Read the trees' mean at the top with care (idea I66).
- **The smoke run is an attempt.** The first end-to-end try (tag `smoke`, MLflow run 3a43d155) counts in versions tried.

## Could be improved
1. **Weekly refits** (I64): the largest misses are whole seasons (R13), and 2023 trains on 2022 alone.
2. **FTN's "0 pass rushers" as missing** (I72), in the next edition: the one input fault the football sanity check found (R17). Personnel isn't on this list any more: even the true personnel adds nothing (R18).
3. **A blend of the trees and the linear rung for the amounts' means** (I66), for the top tenth's over-shoot (R14).
4. **The chain (method B)**: pressure, then sack or throw, then completion, so the outcomes agree with each other.
5. **A shared post-game mode for the harness** (I62), before a fourth post-game expert writes its own runner.

## Not looked at
- **Method B, the chain** (pressure, then sack or throw, then completion, then yards): a later project, per the brief. How much of a sack is the pressure was answered in the exploration only.
- **Weekly refits** (idea I64): every test season is fit once, on the seasons before it.
- **The after-the-season fields** (coverage, routes, personnel from participation): `context-retro`.
- **Interceptions, fumbles, scrambles and a dropback's EPA**: left out by the CEO's call 2 (the exploration found nothing to explain).
- **A player-level or team-level forecast from the context** (does a player's context predict his next game?): the yardstick (`crude-forecaster`) will measure what the summaries add downstream.
- **Isotonic recalibration, or recalibrating every chance**: tried for five chances before the rerun, kept for the two that failed (call 17).

## Don't redo
- **Personnel as a call field, from the map or from anywhere.** The map's leaks (call 14), and even the true after-the-season personnel adds nothing beyond FTN's call fields (R18, the CEO's spot-check). Idea I63 is killed.
- **2022 in pressure or time to throw** without a fix for the provider change.
- **Schemes-v2's traits as play inputs.** Last season's team traits never beat the live call fields (they lose clearly for several outcomes): the play's own calls already say what the team did on it. They might still help a forecast before the play, which this expert doesn't make.
- **The map's named-player check** (`explore/personnel_pins.py`) **and the leak spot-check** (`explore/spotcheck_map_leak.py`): done; the answers are R11 and R18.
