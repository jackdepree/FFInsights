# Hunches: the scoreboard of unconventional bets

> Where PCF went against the usual way, and whether it paid off (CLAUDE.md, "The hunch scoreboard"). The market and the consensus are the average, so our edge comes from bets that are different *and* right. This page counts both.

- **Who adds rows:** the CEO, from each brief's "CEO hunches". **Whose hunch it is (the CEO, 2026-11-08):** this log tests the CEO's domain knowledge. A hunch Claude proposes counts as a hunch only if the CEO edits it significantly; otherwise it is recorded in IDEAS.md as Claude's idea or discovery. The point is not to diminish Claude's ideas but to keep this page a clean measure of the CEO's instinct, so we can learn which kinds of hunches give an edge.
- **When:** a row is added when the bet is made, and its verdict filled in when it's tested. Failed bets stay: they show where to lean on the data.
- **Verdicts:** **held** (beat the usual way on held-out data, or the exploration found the signal it predicted), **failed** (it didn't, or the usual way won), **unclear** (too little data, or mixed), **open** (not tested yet).
- **Two questions beside every verdict (the CEO, 2026-11-15).** **Real?** Is the football claim true: does the thing exist, repeat, belong to who the hunch said? **Pays?** Did it make a forecast better on held-out data, and by how much? A hunch can be right about football and still not improve the forecast it was tried on, usually because a simpler input already carries it. Those rows (real, doesn't pay, or not tested yet) are the ones to try on a different outcome: the CEO's reason for the split is that "a discovery is significant when predicting a different outcome". Rows before H50 were filled in by Claude at retro 1 from each row's own text; the CEO can overrule any of them.
- **The size matters as much as the verdict.** Each verdict gives its numbers, so a small win isn't counted like a large one.
- **A held hunch in an exploration is discovery, not proof.** Only the harness proves a model beats something.
- **Every bet states its odds first (the CEO, 2026-11-15).** From H50 on, before a hunch is tested the CEO writes a confidence (low, medium or high: roughly 60%, 75% or 90% sure it holds; or a percentage, with a chance for each outcome if the CEO sees more than one) and an expected size in the target's units, and Claude writes its own expectation in the next column. Rows before H50 show a dash: nothing is filled in after the result is known (a few of Claude's expectations are in the Size column of H36 to H39). The retro scores both columns ([RETRO.md](RETRO.md)).
- **State the football claim apart from the method.** A hunch about what is true in football and a hunch about how to measure it are separate rows, so the retro can tell which kind pays.
