# Exploration: Validation harness (`validation-harness`)

> A light exploration (infrastructure project). It profiles what the harness touches: when each table part becomes known, what a view of the world at one moment costs, how many steps a walk-forward backtest has, and whether the planned future-deletion check (ARCHITECTURE.md, check 4) catches leaks planted on purpose.
> Run on 2026-11-02 with [`explore/profile_harness.py`](explore/profile_harness.py) on the built tables (1999â€“2026). Lags are measured on 2016â€“2024 (plays: 2024).

**Verdict: GO.** `load(name, at)` already does the gateway's core job (each part is filtered to `known_at` â‰¤ the moment) and is fast enough to call at every prediction moment. The deletion check caught all three planted leaks. One of them was a leak we didn't plant on purpose: a feature written as "earlier games" uses last Monday night's stats, which aren't known at the planned Tuesday-morning moment (F2). Two of the brief's questions come from this: when a prediction is made (F2, F3), and whether features are built at every moment or in bulk (F4, F5).

## Findings

| # | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Parts fall into "known before kickoff" and "known after", and the gap can be months.** Before: schedule (the season's schedule, about 60 days ahead), roster and depth (the start of the week, about 5.5 days ahead), injury (about 2 days ahead), game status and tags (1 hour ahead), pregame lines (at kickoff). After: stats, snaps, results, play-by-play, drives and result tags (+24 h), NGS (median +59 h, up to 7.5 days), FTN (+14 days), PFR (median +8.5 days, up to 13), participation and on-field players (median +92 days, up to 158: after the Super Bowl). Since participation is filtered out by `known_at` alone, an in-season view at any moment already hides the current season's participation. | strong |
| F2 | **A Tuesday 10:00 ET prediction can't see last Monday night's stats.** Stats are known at kickoff + 24 h, so a Monday 8:15 PM game is in at Tuesday 8:15 PM. An "honest" feature, PPR points per game in *earlier games* this season, failed the deletion check at 21 of 22 cutoffs in 2024: 1,663 rows differed, and every one belonged to a player whose last game kicked off under 24 hours before the moment (the Monday games). "Earlier games" is not "known at the moment". | strong |
| F3 | **The week's spreads and totals are known only at kickoff** (`game.pregame`, odds-collection#F3: nflverse's lines are the close). A Tuesday prediction can't use them. The "Vegas line" baseline needs either a moment near kickoff or our collected odds with their own timestamps (ESPN open/close, The Odds API snapshots). | strong |
| F4 | **The deletion check bites, and it's cheap.** Features computed from the world truncated at a cutoff vs from the full world, on the rows whose prediction moment is at or before the cutoff: the feature that includes the current game (cleaning-port#F8) failed 22/22 cutoffs; the feature that filters participation by "earlier weeks" failed 21/22 (week 1 has no earlier week), with 153,106 rows differing; the Monday-night case failed 21/22 (F2). 22 cutoffs on one season took 1.5â€“1.9 seconds. | strong |
| F5 | **A view at one moment is fast but big.** At the Tuesday of 2024 week 10: `player_game` stats and snaps for 2006â€“2024 load in 0.6 s (535,502 rows, 400 MB); all 11 parts in 1.9 s (1.4 GB); play-by-play for 2006â€“2024 in 0.5 s (881k rows, 1.8 GB). A fresh view at every one of 193 moments costs minutes, not hours. Memory is the limit, so a model should declare the parts it reads, not just the tables. | strong |
| F6 | **A 2016â€“2024 in-season backtest has 193 steps:** 21 weeks a season to 2020, 22 from 2021. 38 week windows don't start on a Tuesday (after Thursday- or Saturday-ending weeks, and in the playoffs), so "Tuesday morning" has to be defined as "10:00 ET on the first day of the week window". At that time it's before every week's first game (0 exceptions). | strong |
| F7 | **A few "before" rows arrive late.** 0.05% of injury rows and 0.03% of roster rows are known after kickoff (up to 1.6 days). The gateway hides them at the moment. A feature computed in bulk from the full table wouldn't, which is another reason bulk features must pass the deletion check. | strong |
| F8 | **No revised (snapshotted) source feeds the tables yet.** The players file is used only to map PFR ids to `gsis_id`. The gateway's "latest snapshot at the moment" duty (ARCHITECTURE.md) has no user until a later project adds one (contracts, rosters as state). | strong |
| F9 | **What `load(name, at)` doesn't do yet:** refuse inputs a model didn't declare; read expert outputs and check their `train_end` (model vintage, check 6); snapshots (F8); return a view object that knows its moment and cadence. | strong |
| F10 | **There's no `core/contract.py` yet.** ARCHITECTURE.md gives the Expert interface and the standard output schema to `package-scaffold` and `validation-harness`. The checks run against that interface, so the harness has to build it first. | strong |

## What this means for the brief

- **The checks are practical as designed.** Cheap views (F5) make "a fresh view at every moment" affordable, and the deletion check is fast (F4). Neither needs sampling for cost on a season-by-season model.
- **The prediction moment is a real decision, not a detail** (F2, F3): it decides what every feature may use.
- **Leaks come from timing, not only from obvious mistakes** (F2): only a check against the gateway catches them.
