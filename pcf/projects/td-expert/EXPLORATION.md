# Exploration: the touchdown expert (`td-expert`)

> A first look at the CEO's two hunches (H67: allocation is tied to scheme and play calling; H68: explosive-play rate with red-zone opportunities are the strongest predictors of rest-of-season touchdowns) and five questions (Q1 to Q5: red-zone efficiency from the spread of past plays, touchdown or field goal, touchdown regression, how noisy one touchdown is, how many chances make half a touchdown).
> Run on 2026-11-16 with [`explore/first_look.py`](explore/first_look.py) (output: [`explore/first_look.txt`](explore/first_look.txt)). Regular seasons only; the vault (2025) wasn't read.

**How to read it.**
- **A chance** is a target (RB, WR, TE) or a carry (QB, RB, WR, TE; scrambles included). **Touchdowns** are rushing and receiving, counted for the player who scored. Passing touchdowns for quarterbacks weren't looked at.
- **Expected touchdowns** is the chance that a target or carry scores, from where it happened (yard line, down, distance, throw depth, side, clock, score), from boosted trees fit only on earlier seasons. 400,573 chances, 2012 to 2024, 15,986 touchdowns. It is calibrated: targets scored 10,275 against 9,962 expected, carries 5,711 against 5,660.
- **Rest of season:** after weeks 4, 8 and 12, a player's touchdowns in the weeks left, forecast from his games so far and last season. 9,705 player rows (2+ games, 2+ chances a game, at least one game afterwards), 4,942 of them scored (2019 to 2024, each from models fit on earlier seasons from 2012). The average player scored 2.12 more touchdowns, with a spread of 2.42.
- **"Removes X%"** is the share of squared error removed against the plain average, on held-out seasons. "Adds X%" is the share of the *remaining* error removed when something is added to a forecast. Intervals are 90%, resampling whole team-seasons (or weeks, for drives).
- **Team level:** 608 team half-seasons (weeks 1 to 9 against the rest), 2006 to 2024; a **red-zone trip** is a drive with a play inside the 20.
- Discovery only: one rough version, not through the gateway or the harness.

**Verdict: GO, with a narrower job than the hunches hoped.** Touchdowns can be forecast about as well as a player's volume can (32% of the error), and nothing tested here beat plain yards and chances by more than noise. What the expert can add is different from a better count: a calibrated worth for every chance, a regression measure that works (a player keeps about a tenth of his touchdowns over expected), an honest size for the luck (28% of the error remains even knowing every chance he gets), and the chance of at least one touchdown. The team's tendencies add a little for receivers and tight ends (H67).

## The noise ceiling (measured first)

| Reference | Result |
|---|---|
| **What his games so far remove** (14 measures a game, this season and last) | 32.1% (29.4 to 34.6). RB 38%, WR 30%, QB 25%, TE 16% |
| **Fitting noise** (the same model refit three times on resampled training rows) | 0.29 points. Differences under about 0.6 points aren't read |
| **Hindsight: how many games he then played** | 32.1% rises to 44.7%. Availability is a third of the room |
| **Hindsight: every chance he then got** (the sum of its expected touchdowns) | 72.4% (70.3 to 74.2). So 40 points of the error are about which chances he will get |
| **What is left even then** | 27.6% of the error is whether the chances he got were scored. Pure dice on those chances would leave 34.5%, so there is little hidden skill inside it |
| **Split halves** (odd weeks against even, 2,386 player-seasons with 50+ chances) | Touchdowns repeat at 0.43; expected touchdowns at 0.60; touchdowns over expected at 0.05 (0.02 to 0.08) |

- **The room is in forecasting chances, not in valuing them.** Between the 32% a forecast gets and the 72% hindsight gets lies opportunity: who plays, how much, and where on the field.
- **Expected touchdowns are a steadier record than touchdowns** (0.60 against 0.43), and what a player scores beyond them is almost entirely luck (0.05).

## The case against this exploration

- **Squared error on a count.** Touchdowns are small counts; a model built for counts, scored on the chance of zero, one or two, may rank the inputs differently. The 0.6-point rule for reading differences is for this scoring only.
- **Only players who played again.** Rows need a game after the cut, so the forecast was never asked who gets hurt or benched. Hindsight on games played is worth 12.6 points; a forecast of availability would be worth some part of that.
- **Rest of season only.** The next game alone wasn't scored, and it is the horizon that props and start/sit use. Red-zone role may matter more there (F4's red-zone gain goes from −0.8% after week 4 to +0.6% after week 12).
- **Expected touchdowns know nothing about the team.** The worth of a chance ignores how good the offense and defense are, so some of "finishing" in F5 and F10 is a player's team.
- **H67 used five blunt tendencies.** Schemes-v2's traits (personnel, motion, formation) weren't used, and the test for "tied to" was forecasting, not description: the correlations in F16 (−0.16, +0.13) say the tie exists and is modest.
- **Team-level tests are straight lines on 608 half-seasons**, with 18 touchdowns a half. A weak effect on the split would be hidden by the dice.
- **Passing touchdowns weren't looked at**, nor defensive and return touchdowns.
- **Pairs were ranked by the same data that picked them**, so "the best pair" is flattered; the ranking of the CEO's pair (59th) isn't affected by that.

## What this means for the brief

- **The expert's distinct outputs are measures, not a better count:** expected touchdowns per chance and per game; touchdowns over expected with how much to keep (F5); the luck in a game or a season (F7); the chance of at least one touchdown.
- **The forecast's centre should start from volume** (yards and chances), with the team's offense and tendencies as small additions (F16) and red-zone carries for quarterbacks (F4).
- **Forecasting chances is where the room is** (the ceiling): availability and opportunity, which are `participation`'s and `opportunity-quality`'s jobs.
- **Drop from the team side:** the spread of red-zone plays (F11) and a touchdown-or-field-goal model (F13); red-zone efficiency comes from the offense rating (F12).
- **Open for the CEO:** what the expert forecasts and for whom, the next-game horizon, passing touchdowns, the no-noise test, and the method.
