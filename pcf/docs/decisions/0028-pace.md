# 0028: Pace measures every play against PCF's own expected values, and forecasts volume from them

**Date:** 2026-11-10 · **Decided by:** the CEO agreed the brief ("let's build the pace (team) expert as is", 2026-11-10: team level only, players later in `player-pace`). The calls below are Claude's technical calls, listed for veto at merge. · **Project:** `pace`

**Decision:**
- **Two new table parts** (`pcf.data.tables.pace`):
  - `team_game.pace`: each team's volume and habits in a game. These are plays, dropbacks, runs, drives and plays per drive. Pass rate over expected is measured on every play, and also on 1st, 2nd and 3rd down apart. Pace over expected (seconds; negative is fast) is measured on every snap with a gap. Also the no-huddle rate, EPA per play, and the defense's versions.
  - `play.pace`: each snap's expected pass chance and expected gap, kept for `player-pace`.
  - Both are known when play-by-play is (kickoff + 1 day), and both are after-kickoff parts.
- **PCF's own expected models,** fit for each season on the seasons before it.
- **The expert `pace`** (`pcf.experts.pace`), with 39 variants:
  - per habit, the league, last-4 and season averages and schemes-v2's blend;
  - per volume measure, three averages, the usual way, and the full-sample way.

**The calls:**
1. **Expected pass chance and expected gap are gradient-boosted trees** (scikit-learn's histogram version: 300 rounds, 63 leaves, learning rate 0.1, seed 0).
   - **Training:** fit on up to the 10 seasons before the one they measure, and never fewer than 3. So the first season with pace measures is 2002.
   - **Expected pass inputs:** the situation only: down, distance, field position, quarter, the half's and the game's clock, the score, and nflverse's and Vegas's win chances.
   - **Expected gap inputs:** win chance, expected points, quarter, the half's clock, what stopped the clock since the last snap (an incompletion, out of bounds, a penalty or no-play, a spike, a sack) and timeouts called.
   - **Why:** the exploration's recipe (pace#F4, F13). nflverse's `xpass` was fit in-sample before 2020 and now runs 1–2 points high.
2. **A missing input is kept as missing** (the trees handle it), so no play is dropped. 4% of 2023's plays have no Vegas win chance.
3. **What counts:**
   - **Snaps:** runs, passes, no-plays, kneels and spikes with a down. Scrimmage plays exclude kneels and spikes. A dropback counts sacks and scrambles.
   - **The gap:** game seconds from the previous snap in the same drive, quarter and possession, 0–60 s.
   - **Playoffs:** included in the parts. The consistency numbers use the regular season only.
4. **Fitted models are cached** in the interim zone, one file per season, keyed by a code version (`MODEL_VERSION`) and the seasons fit on.
   - **Refits:** a change to either refits. A revision nflverse makes to an old season doesn't; bump the version to refit.
   - **Checks:** each pair is checked to have been fit only on earlier seasons before it's used.
5. **Names kept apart from existing columns:**
   - `def_dropbacks_all`, since `tendencies` has `def_dropbacks` for neutral plays only;
   - `pcf_xpass` and `pcf_pass_oe`, since nflverse's play-by-play has its own `pass_oe`.
6. **Schemes-v2's history averages are reused, not copied.** Its `_features` now takes the known-at column of the part it reads (the default is unchanged, and its tests pass).
   - **The registry:** pace's `depends_on` drops `schemes-v2`, because pace reads no schemes output. The contract requires a model dependency to be read.
   - **Still to do:** moving these averages into a shared module is the cleaner home, left for when a third expert needs them.
7. **The volume forecast is a weighted least squares** (rung 1). It uses:
   - the team's own volume history (gaps from the league);
   - what the opposing defense allows;
   - **no line:** nflverse's lines are known only at kickoff, after the Saturday run. The first build read them with a fallback for an unknown line, and every Saturday row got the fallback, so they're removed; the predictions are the same (pace R5).
   - both offenses' and defenses' EPA per play and plays per drive (pace#F9);
   - both teams' habits: the neutral ones in `<v>_neutral` (the usual way), or the full-sample ones in `<v>_full`.

   `<v>_full` is judged against `<v>_neutral`, which makes the brief's test of H22 a like-for-like swap. The structural drives × plays-per-drive rung waits for this one's result.
8. **Consistency is measured on gaps from each season's league level.** Pace over expected runs +3 s above zero in 2006 and about 0 from 2010, because the early clock data differs from the seasons each model was fit on. Pooled raw, that shared drift reads as teams repeating: the defense's pace over expected scored 0.80 raw, but only 0.08 centred. The features already work in gaps from the league's level, so they're unaffected.

**Alternatives:**
- **nflverse's `xpass`:** it is in-sample before 2020 and drifts after (pace#F13).
- **One expected model fit on every season:** that leaks later seasons into earlier measures.
- **Logistic regression for the expected pass chance:** the trees agree with `xpass` at r 0.988 in the exploration, and the clock interactions in the gap need trees.
- **Reading schemes-v2's published forecasts:** no expert reads another's outputs yet, the output paths don't match `view.output` yet, and the habits pace needs are its own.
