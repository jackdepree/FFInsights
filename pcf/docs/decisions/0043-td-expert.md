# 0043: td-expert, the build's technical calls

**Date:** 2026-11-24 · **Decided by:** Claude (technical calls, listed for the CEO's veto at merge), inside the agreed brief and the CEO's go of 2026-11-24 ("Yes, start td-expert") · **Project:** `td-expert`

## The facts (layer 0 parts)

1. **Four new table parts, not a new table** (decision 0008's pattern): `play.xtd` (each chance's expected touchdown), `drive.td_chance` (each drive's chance of a touchdown at its first snap, its first snap inside the 20, and its best moment), `player_game.touchdowns` and `team_game.touchdowns` (the sums, the luck measure, near misses). Prefix `tdx_`, so nothing clashes with play-by-play's `td_team` or `td_player_id`. All known when the play-by-play is (kickoff + 1 day). `build_player_game` now ends with them, after opportunity-quality's.
2. **A chance is a target or a carry by anyone,** quarterbacks included; two-point tries out. **A carry is any run with a rusher, scrambles included.** nflverse marks a scramble rush 0 and pass 1, so a `rush == 1` filter misses them: 32 of 2023's 496 rushing touchdowns. With them in, the parts' rushing touchdowns equal the season stats' (496 = 496). The same filter in opportunity-quality only matters for QBs, which it doesn't score; it's a pitfall, not a fix to make there.
3. **Expected touchdowns reuse opportunity-quality's where it has them** (RB, WR and TE targets and carries: `oq_td_exp`). **QB carries get their own model** (opportunity-quality's carry inputs plus a scramble flag; trees with its settings), because a QB's carry is a different play (sneaks, scrambles, designed runs at the goal line). Other chances (a fullback's, a QB's catch) use opportunity-quality's fitted models on their inputs. **A throw's expected touchdowns is its target's**, credited to the passer.
4. **The drive model** is a trees classifier on the situation at a snap (yard line, down, distance, goal to go, quarter, clock, score), trained on every snap of earlier seasons' drives, labelled by whether the drive ended in a touchdown. It gives three numbers per drive: at the first snap (`p_start`), at the first snap inside the 20 (`p_rz`), and its highest (`p_peak`, the near miss). Fit from 2006 on up to 10 earlier seasons; fits cached per season with a model version, as opportunity-quality's are.
5. **The drive-aware hindsight** (`tdx_hind`), the team's ceiling: a drive that reached the 20 counts its chance there; a drive that didn't counts what happened (a long touchdown is a touchdown). It knows how many drives and trips there were, and leaves the dice from the 20 in. The brief asked for it instead of summing every chance's expected touchdowns, which counts a drive's touchdown many times over (the exploration's F21).
6. **The luck measure:** touchdowns over expected in dice spreads, z = (td − xtd)/√Σp(1−p), and the exact chance of at least that many from those chances (Poisson-binomial, vectorized). Looking back only.
7. **Near misses are a description, never an input** (the brief): a field-goal drive's highest chance of a touchdown.

## The forecast

8. **A new kind, `count`,** in the harness: the forecast is a mean number of touchdowns; the main score is the Poisson log loss (−log P(y) under a Poisson count with that mean), the readable score the squared error; calibration judges the chance of 1+ and of 2+ under a Poisson around the mean, each as a probability (VALIDATION.md's bars); the q10-q90 range is Poisson's and reported, not judged (a count's range is too coarse to cover 80% exactly). The sanity rules: quantiles ordered, mean not negative.
9. **Three families** (players' rushing and receiving touchdowns, a QB's thrown touchdowns, a team's offensive touchdowns) **times two horizons** (next game; the rest of the regular season). Next game assumes he plays (only played games are scored); the rest of the season is the sum over his team's remaining regular-season games, a game he misses counting 0, its exposure the games left. A row is scored only when its whole window is known.
10. **The ladder:** rung 0 `rate` (his touchdowns per game over this season and last, shrunk toward his position's rate, times the exposure) → method B `volume` (a Poisson regression on volume: yards, chances, snaps, his touchdowns, playing time from participation's inputs, the team's touchdowns and offense, the opponent's touchdowns allowed) → method A `chances` (two stages) → trees on B's inputs (players, next game: `skill_next_volume_trees`).
11. **Method A, two stages:** stage 1 forecasts next game's expected touchdowns from his chances (by kind and by zone: inside the 20, the 10, the 5, end-zone targets), playing time and the team's; its target is the next row's `last_xtd`, read by joining each training row to its successor (so it only uses training rows). Stage 2 is a Poisson regression on corrections (his touchdowns over expected per chance, shrunk with 100 chances; the offense's efficiency, calling and goal-to-go pass rate; the opponent's) with exposure × stage 1 as the offset.
12. **The Poisson regressions** are sklearn's `PoissonRegressor` on standardized inputs, the penalty picked on the latest 20% of the training window, the exposure applied as rate y/e weighted by e. Saturday run, refit once a season, training from 2013 (the first season with a full year of expected touchdowns behind it), reported 2016-2024, the vault (2025) opened once at the end.
13. **The side tests ride on B,** as variants that differ by one group of inputs: I58 (a QB's red-zone carries), H68 (red-zone chances, non-QBs), H67 (the offense's calling: B against B without it). They are walked on B's saved features (decision 0041's walks) with 90% intervals by week.
14. **The yardstick doesn't apply** (it is yards only and market-free: decision 0042). Touchdowns' baseline is rung 0, the season rate; there is no free history of touchdown prices.

## The regression measure

15. **The measure:** a player's touchdowns over expected per chance in a regular season (thrown: per throw), and the share k of it he keeps the next season, one k per position, least squares on the 6 season pairs before the tested season. Scored on next season's touchdowns over expected (squared error) against k = 1 and k = 0, with 90% intervals from whole players resampled. 30+ chances (150+ throws) the season before.

## Validation

16. **The signal test's caveat:** stage 1's target is built inside `fit` from the training rows, so the harness's shuffled-outcome refits shuffle stage 2's target only. That still tests what A adds over its own volume, and the leakage checks see stage 1's construction (it reads only training rows).
17. **Shipping rule (the brief's):** A ships if it ties or beats B on the Poisson log loss; else B ships.

## After the results

18. **The mean is recalibrated** on every fitted rung (B, A, trees; not rung 0): a Poisson fit of the outcome on L = log(mean) and L² with log(mean) as the offset, on the latest 20% of the training window from a model fit on the earlier 80% (the penalty's own rule), L held inside the range it saw, a small ridge toward no change. The first players' run failed the chance of 1+ (error 1.55 points, slope 1.12): the Poisson regression was too high at both ends (the quietest players 0.078 said, 0.056 scored; the top 1.03 said, 0.72 scored) and low in the middle, a bend a single slope can't fix. After it: error 0.26, slope 1.01. Every rung's comparisons are on recalibrated means, so the ladder stays fair.
19. **Stage 1 keeps the training rows' order.** Leakage check 8 failed for thrown touchdowns (108 rows at 2016 week 1 moved when later results were scrambled). The cause wasn't a leak: `stage_one`'s `unique` and join didn't keep row order, and the penalty is picked on the latest 20% by position, so the same rows in a new order gave a slightly different fit. Fixed (`maintain_order`), with a test; check 8 then passed. The players' A runs before the fix keep their scorecards (check 8 passed for them); their reported comparisons come from walks rerun after it (the same to 0.02%).
20. **20 shuffled copies, not 100,** for every run after the first (the harness's 20-of-20 rule): each copy now refits twice (the recalibration), and the first run's 100 of 100 showed the margin.
21. **The champions** (the shipping rule, then the ladder): players next game **`skill_next_volume_trees`** (A ties B, +0.03%; trees beat B by 0.57% and A by 0.54%: the ladder's next rung beat the one below); players rest of season **`skill_rest_volume+qb_rz`** (B beats A by 1.25%; QBs' red-zone carries add 0.37%, I58); thrown next game **`thrown_next_chances`** (A beats B, interval clear); thrown rest of season **`thrown_rest_volume`** (B beats A by 10.1%); teams next game **`team_next_volume`** (B beats A by 0.19%); teams rest of season **`team_rest_volume`**: A ties B (−0.29%, −0.64 to +0.07) but doesn't clearly beat rung 0, while B does (+0.88%), so the tie goes to the simpler rung (rule k). **A call for the CEO's veto:** the brief's rule ("A at least as good as B") would ship A here.
22. **The rest-of-season and team chances aren't published as calibrated.** Under a Poisson count around the mean they fail (players' 1+ error 8 points, thrown 16, teams' 2+ 5): the means are the product there; a spread that fits is idea I116 (many games) and I119 (one game's 2+).
23. **The vault** is each champion's harness run with 2025 opened once, plus walks of the other rungs on that run's saved features and the regression measure tested on 2025. Done 2026-11-24: every champion passes all 10 checks and 20 of 20 shuffled copies; teams next game ties rung 0; A beat B for players next game (+0.46%); I58 reversed on QBs' rest of season (−5.1%) (RESULTS R15).
24. **The champions stand after the vault.** The vault reports; it doesn't re-pick (docs/VALIDATION.md: a model changed after it is "vault spent"). Dropping I58 from the rest-of-season champion, or shipping A for players next game, would each spend the vault and leave the live 2026 ledger as the only test. **A call for the CEO's veto**, with I58 the likeliest one to drop: nine seasons said +10.6% on QBs, one said −15.6%. Opportunity-quality went the other way on its regression measure (its RESULTS R17: the vault's pick shipped); there the two methods were close in the backtest (1.2%), here the backtest is clear over nine seasons of QBs.
